Let's solve the highest employee ID problem using the `DENSE_RANK()` window function.

While `ROW_NUMBER()` and `DENSE_RANK()` look very similar, using `DENSE_RANK()` provides an incredibly important safety net when it comes to ties (duplicate values).

---

## The SQL Query

```sql
WITH RankedEmployees AS (
    SELECT 
        empId,
        first_name,
        last_name,
        department,
        DENSE_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY empId DESC) AS rank_num
    FROM employee
)
SELECT 
    empId, 
    first_name, 
    last_name, 
    department
FROM RankedEmployees
WHERE rank_num = 1;

```

---

## Why use `DENSE_RANK()` instead of `ROW_NUMBER()`?

If your `empId` column is a strict primary key (meaning it is impossible to have duplicate IDs), `ROW_NUMBER()` and `DENSE_RANK()` will give you the exact same result.

**However, if you are ranking by a column that *can* have ties—like finding the highest salary, or the highest test score—`DENSE_RANK()` behaves much more intelligently.**

### The Tie-Breaker Difference:

Imagine you have three employees competing for the top spot:

* Alice: $100k
* Bob: $100k
* Charlie: $90k

Here is how the different functions assign numbers when ordering by salary descending:

| Employee | Salary | `ROW_NUMBER()` | `RANK()` | `DENSE_RANK()` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Alice** | $100k | **1** | **1** | **1** |
| **Bob** | $100k | 2 *(Randomly chosen)* | **1** | **1** |
| **Charlie** | $90k | 3 | 3 *(Skips 2)* | 2 *(Dense/No gaps)* |

### Why `DENSE_RANK()` wins for "Highest" queries:

* **`ROW_NUMBER()`** forces a unique number. If Alice and Bob tie for the highest salary, it will arbitrarily pick one to be `1` and the other to be `2`. Your final query (`WHERE row_num = 1`) would completely leave Bob out.
* **`DENSE_RANK()`** assigns the exact same rank to ties. Both Alice and Bob get a rank of `1`. When you filter by `WHERE rank_num = 1`, **your query safely returns both employees** who share the highest spot.
* It is called "dense" because it never skips a number. Charlie gets a `2` right after the `1`s, unlike standard `RANK()`, which would skip to `3`.
